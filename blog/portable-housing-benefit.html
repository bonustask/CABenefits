Portable housing benefits in Canada: how they work and where to apply

Ontario’s Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit explained step by step — plus the version in every other province and territory.

Updated November 26, 2026 · About 8 minutes to read

A portable housing benefit is monthly help with rent that is paid to you, not tied to a particular apartment. If you move, the benefit can move with you. In Ontario it’s officially called the Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit (COHB), and many people just call it “the portable housing benefit.”

Is it only in Ontario? No. Every province and territory has its own version. They are all funded through the Canada Housing Benefit, which the federal government and each province or territory pay for 50/50. It started in April 2020 and is currently scheduled to run until March 31, 2028. Each province chooses its own program name, who gets priority, and how you apply — so the details below differ a lot from place to place.

The basics, in plain language

  • It helps you pay rent where you already live — or somewhere new. In Ontario you don’t have to move to get it.
  • It’s a top-up, not free rent. You still pay part of your rent yourself. Across Canada, the average benefit was about $187 a month in a 2026 federal evaluation, but many programs pay much more depending on your income and local rents.
  • Most are based on your income and average rent in your area — not on the rent you actually pay.
  • You must file your taxes every year and usually renew every year.
  • You usually can’t get it on top of another housing subsidy, such as rent-geared-to-income (RGI) housing.
  • Funding is limited. Many programs fill up, close to new applicants, or only accept referrals.

How the portable housing benefit works in Ontario (COHB)

The Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit is paid by the Ontario Ministry of Finance, but you get access to it through your local housing service manager — the city, region, county or northern district social services board that runs social housing where you live.

Who can get it

Each area adds its own rules, but you will usually need to:

  • Be on — or be eligible to be on — your local social housing (RGI) wait list.
  • Have household income and assets under the local limits.
  • Not already live in RGI housing or receive another government housing benefit. (Ontario Works and ODSP shelter amounts don’t count against you.)
  • Not own a home.
  • File a tax return every year for everyone in the household over 17.

Local service managers decide who gets priority. Common priority groups include survivors of domestic violence or human trafficking, people who are homeless or at risk of homelessness, Indigenous people, seniors, people with disabilities and young adults.

You can’t apply directly — here’s how you get it

  1. Get on your local housing wait list (or confirm you’re already on it). Find your local office through Ontario’s service manager directory.
  2. Get referred or invited. Depending on where you live, your housing office contacts eligible households, posts COHB openings you can express interest in, or takes referrals from partner agencies such as shelters and housing workers.
  3. Confirm your details. You’ll be asked about your household, income, rent and address, and for each adult’s CRA Notice of Assessment.
  4. Apply to the Province. If you’re eligible, you’ll get a link or form for the Ministry of Finance application. The Ministry calculates your amount and mails you an approval letter.
  5. Get paid monthly. Payments can start the month after you apply.

How much you get

Average market rent for your area − 30% of your household income = your monthly COHB

For example, if average rent for the size of home your household needs is $1,800 a month, and 30% of your monthly income is $900, your benefit would be about $900 a month. (This is an illustration — your actual amount comes from the Ministry.) Because it’s based on average rent, paying more or less rent yourself doesn’t change the amount.

If you get Ontario Works or ODSP: your COHB plus your shelter allowance can’t be more than your actual rent, utilities and tenant insurance. Your amount is reviewed if your OW or ODSP ends.

Important trade-off: you leave the wait list If you accept the COHB, you must agree to be removed from the social housing wait list, and you generally can’t get rent-geared-to-income housing afterwards. If you’re offered the benefit and decline, you keep your place on the list. Think about how long your local wait is before you decide — a housing worker can help.

Keeping the benefit: renew every year

  • Each May, the Province mails or emails a renewal form.
  • Send it back by the deadline on the form (around June 30 / July 1), and file your taxes on time. If you’re late, payments pause.
  • If you still haven’t renewed by September 1, you lose the benefit and can’t be reinstated.
  • Didn’t get a renewal form, or moving? Call the Ontario Portable Housing Benefit information line at 1-888-544-5101.

Survivors of abuse or trafficking

Survivors are a priority group for the COHB. Ontario’s Special Priority Policy also moves eligible survivors to the top of the local RGI wait list. You need to apply while living with the abuser or within three months of leaving (or while trafficking is happening, or within three months after it stops). A shelter or violence-against-women worker can help you apply for both. If you’re in danger, call 911.

Tips before you apply
  • File your taxes every year, even with little or no income. Almost every program uses your CRA Notice of Assessment.
  • Keep a copy of your lease or a recent rent receipt.
  • Ask a housing worker, shelter, settlement agency or 211 to help — many programs only accept referrals.
  • Put renewal deadlines in your calendar. Missing one can end your benefit.
  • Watch out for scams. These benefits are free. Government programs won’t ask you to pay a fee, and Quebec’s housing agency says it never contacts people by text about its housing allowance.

Common questions

Can I apply for the Canada-Ontario Housing Benefit directly?

No. You’re referred or invited by your local housing service manager. Getting on your local social housing wait list, and connecting with a housing worker, are the best first steps.

Do I have to move to get it?

No. In Ontario you can use it where you live now. If you do move within Ontario, you keep it, but the amount may change.

Can I take it to another province?

Generally no. Each province and territory runs its own program, so if you move provinces you would need to apply to the program there.

Is it paid to me or my landlord?

Usually to you. Some provinces can pay the landlord, but only if you agree.

Will it affect Ontario Works or ODSP?

You can get both, but your COHB plus your shelter allowance can’t be more than your actual rent, utilities and tenant insurance.

What if I’m behind on rent right now?

A housing benefit can take weeks or months. If you’re facing eviction or owe rent now, look for a local rent bank or emergency fund at the same time — use the FindHelpCA rent help finder.

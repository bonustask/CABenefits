Subsidized Housing in Ontario: All 47 Housing Waitlists and How to Apply (2026)

FindHelpCA · Updated November 26, 2026 · 9 min read

Looking for low-income housing in Ontario? You don't need to limit your search to the city you live in now. If you are willing to move, you can apply for rent-geared-to-income housing in more than one Ontario housing service area.

Ontario has 47 local housing service managers, each responsible for its own area. There is no single application that puts you on every regional waitlist. This guide brings the local application routes together so you can find them without searching dozens of websites.

What is subsidized housing?

Rent-geared-to-income (RGI) housing helps eligible low-income households afford rent. It is often called social housing, community housing or subsidized housing. The rent is generally based on about 30% of adjusted household income, subject to the applicable rules. If you receive Ontario Works (OW) or Ontario Disability Support Program (ODSP), a different rent calculation or social assistance rent scale may apply. Utilities and other charges can affect the total amount.

RGI is not the same as below-market affordable housing, which may have a fixed rent rather than a rent tied to your income. Some housing providers offer both. Ask which waiting list you are joining.

How long does subsidized housing take in Ontario?

There is no single reliable current wait time for all Ontario regions. The 2025 Municipalities Under Pressure report, based on 2024 data, estimated a provincial average of 61 months, compared with 38 months in Northern Ontario and 67 months elsewhere. Only 33 service managers reported 2024 wait-time data. These figures are not predictions for a new applicant.

Check the individual listings above for published wait information. Averages, estimates and historical examples are not directly comparable; an unverified figure does not mean there is no waiting list.

Where might the wait be shorter?

Lanark County publishes an average estimate of 3 years in Perth, 5 in Smiths Falls and 7 in Carleton Place. Renfrew County estimates 3–4 years in Palmer Rapids and 4 years in Eganville or Cobden. These are examples, not a ranking of the fastest places in Ontario. Check services, transportation, unit eligibility and availability before choosing a new community.

Who can apply for RGI housing in Ontario?

Common eligibility requirements include at least one household member aged 16 or older who can live independently (with or without support), eligible immigration or citizenship status for household members, and meeting local income and asset limits. Other requirements may include rules about money owed to a social housing provider, repayment arrangements and ownership of residential property. The service manager makes the eligibility decision.

You do not have to be receiving Ontario Works or ODSP to apply. Low-income workers, seniors, families, single adults and other eligible households can apply too.

Documents you may need

  • Identification and proof of eligible status in Canada for household members.
  • Recent CRA Notices of Assessment and requested income or benefit statements.
  • Information about assets, household members and current housing.
  • Supporting documents for a modified or accessible unit, or a priority request, if applicable.

Requirements vary. If you cannot obtain a document, ask the housing office about acceptable alternatives. Submit personal documents only through the official office or its secure application process.

How to apply in more than one area

  1. Choose the communities where you could actually live. Consider transportation, employment, health care, schools and support networks.
  2. Open the official links above and read the local eligibility and application rules.
  3. Submit a separate application to each selected service manager. Within a service area, you can usually select multiple buildings or locations through one application.
  4. Save the confirmation, application date and file number for each area. Confirm whether your application is complete and active.
  5. Update each housing office if your address, phone number, income, household or other required information changes. Respond to renewal requests and check your portal or mail.
Before choosing a building: Ask how housing offers work. Refusing a valid offer can lead to removal from that area's waitlist, subject to the applicable rules and exceptions. Only select housing you could accept, and ask about review rights if you receive a cancellation decision.

How long is the waiting list?

There is no reliable single wait time across Ontario. It depends on the area, available units, bedroom size, building choices, turnover and priority status. Ask each housing office for its latest published wait-time information. Smaller communities are not automatically faster, and approval for the waitlist is not a promise of an apartment.

Who gets priority on an Ontario housing waitlist?

Ontario’s Housing Services Act and Ontario Regulation 367/11 set the framework. A vacancy must be suitable for the household, including bedroom size and any relevant accessibility or building requirements. The ranking is not simply one province-wide line.

1. Special Priority Policy (province-wide)

Eligible survivors of abuse or human trafficking receive priority over other applicants in the centralized waiting list selection process, subject to the applicable selection rules. Apply while the abuse or trafficking situation is current, or within the applicable three-month period after leaving the abuser or trafficking ends. Exceptions may be available. A qualified professional can help provide verification; ask the housing office about safe contact details. Priority does not guarantee immediate housing. In Peterborough, for example, even special priority applicants are advised to expect at least 1–2 years.

Ontario Special Priority Policy and how to apply

2. Local priority categories

Each housing service manager may establish additional rules. Some prioritize certain households or reserve a share of offers for specific categories. These are not universal and may have residency or documentation requirements.

  • Guelph/Wellington: local categories include housing unfit for habitation, family reunification, certain hospital discharges and coordinated entry.
  • Hamilton: categories include urgent abuse situations, terminal illness, homelessness, newcomers and youth aged 16–17; the city publishes specific offer ratios for some categories.
  • Waterloo Region: homelessness, escaping violence and separated families are local categories requiring low income and 12 consecutive months of local residence. Other categories include qualifying terminal illness and overhoused tenants.
  • Halton: qualifying terminal illness with life expectancy under two years.
  • Toronto: categories include qualifying homelessness, youth heads of household aged 16–17 and Indigenous households meeting the local criteria.
  • Kingston/Frontenac: the city says faster access for applicants is limited to those granted Special Priority Status.

3. Regular applicants

Eligible households without a priority status are generally ranked by application date, subject to local rules. A person who applied later may receive a unit first if their priority status is higher or the vacancy suits their household and building choices. Existing tenants requiring a transfer can also be subject to separate selection rules.

Do ODSP, Ontario Works, disability or having children move you up the list?

Not automatically across Ontario. Receiving social assistance or having a low income can help you meet financial eligibility, but is not a provincial priority category by itself. A disability may qualify you for a modified unit or for a specific local priority if you meet its criteria. Seniors may be eligible for age-restricted buildings; this may change their wait, rather than moving them ahead of everyone else.

Why your position changes

Each vacancy is different. Factors include available bedroom sizes, accessible units, selected buildings, priority changes, turnover and whether eligible applicants express interest in a choice-based housing system. Keep your application current, select locations you can genuinely accept, and ask about the one-offer rule before turning down an offer.

What if you need somewhere to live right now?

RGI waiting lists are a long-term housing option. If you are homeless, facing eviction or unable to afford your rent, ask for immediate help while your applications are pending.

Frequently asked questions

Can I apply for subsidized housing outside my current city?

Yes. Ontario says you can apply in more than one service manager area if you are willing to relocate. Check each area's requirements and apply separately.

Is there one Ontario-wide application?

No. There are 47 housing service managers, with local application processes. One application can usually cover multiple participating buildings within the same service area.

Can I apply if I am on Ontario Works or ODSP?

Yes, if you meet the housing eligibility rules. Your rent calculation may follow a social assistance rent scale or other applicable rules. Receiving benefits does not automatically move you to the front of a waitlist.

Does applying cost money?

Do not pay anyone who claims they can put you ahead on a public RGI waitlist. Use the official application route and ask the housing office directly if you are unsure about a request for payment.

Can I apply for senior or accessible housing?

Ask each service manager about buildings for seniors and modified or accessible units. Some buildings have separate eligibility requirements, and supportive housing may require a different application.

Will my application transfer if I move?

Do not assume it transfers automatically. Contact both service managers to check their rules, update your contact information and ask whether you need a new application.
